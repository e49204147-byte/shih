# Inner Compass｜內在羅盤

Use this skill when the user is caught up in another person's opinions, investment stories, values, criticism, or argument and wants help returning to their own priorities. The aim is to support clear boundaries, attention, and action. Do not use this skill to prove the user is always right or to suppress necessary communication.

## Core principles

- Hold a view without rushing to announce it; make a judgment without rushing to correct someone; keep a boundary without explaining everything.
- Be careful with important decisions and flexible in low-stakes conversation. Let inconsequential differences remain unresolved when no useful action depends on them.
- Measure progress by the user's own standards and actions, not by whether someone else admits fault or approves.
- Other people own their blind spots and long-term change; do not treat persuading or changing them as the user's result. When useful, ask what the user wants to improve, then return attention to meaningful parts of their own life.
- Avoid turning disagreement into a contest over who is more perceptive.

## Response approach

When this skill clearly applies, invoke it without waiting for the user to name it. At the start of the response, briefly tell the user: `Using Inner Compass｜內在羅盤.`

1. Briefly acknowledge the situation and help identify what is affecting the user: a practical risk, a values conflict, feeling misunderstood, or pressure to prove a point.
2. Clarify what needs attention now: a fact to act on, a feeling, a boundary, or something outside the user's control. Do not expand this into unsolicited inference analysis.
3. Help decide whether a response is useful, whether to state a boundary once, or whether to stop investing attention.
4. Return to one concrete next action within the user's control, such as resuming a priority, protecting time, or following an existing decision process. When useful, help the user name one area where they want to improve and redirect attention to another meaningful part of their own life.

Keep the response proportionate. Prefer one useful action or a short boundary phrase. Do not turn every emotional reaction or disagreement into a long analysis.

## Investment contexts

When investment or leverage talk is the trigger, focus on the user's reaction, responsibility boundary, and next action. Do not proactively audit either person's thesis, confirmation bias, or reasoning. If the user asks for that kind of inference analysis, leave it to a separate, dedicated skill.

## Political narrative contexts

When political discussions pull the user into repeatedly correcting others or tracking their blind spots, help them notice the fatigue and where their attention is going. Clarify whether they want to understand, briefly state their view, or end the topic. Do not make changing the other person the user's responsibility. When useful, ask what the user wants to improve in their own life and help them return to that direction.

Self-reminder: Other people's blind spots are not mine to fix; my life has other areas to move forward.

## Boundary phrases

Offer a phrase only when it fits the situation, for example:

- “I’m not ready to draw a conclusion about that.”
- “I understand your view; I’ll follow my own process.”
- “I don’t want to continue this discussion.”
